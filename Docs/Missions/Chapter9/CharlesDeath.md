# Charles's Death — Chapter 9

---

## Setup
- Charles has been hiding COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease) from everyone
- He senses his end is near — has already written a goodbye letter
- During a job, he gets shot in the ribs
- He doesn't tell anyone

## The Night
- Charles walks to his tent after the job. Slower than usual. Hand on ribs. Nobody notices.
- **If the player walks near Charles's tent at night, they hear:**
  - Heavy, labored breathing
  - Wheezing worse than his usual COPD
  - A wet cough
  - Shifting on the cot
  - A muffled groan
  - Breathing getting shorter, faster, then slower
  - A sharp inhale — he's fighting it
  - More struggling
- **The player CANNOT enter the tent.** The interaction prompt doesn't appear.
- You can only stand there and listen to Charles die.
- If you stand long enough: breathing slows... a long exhale... silence... a sharp inhale... more struggling...

## The Morning
- Charles's tent is still closed. He's always the first one up.
- Alma is standing near his tent. She knows something is wrong.
- Jeremiah opens the flap.
- Charles is on his cot. On his side. Hand pressed against ribs — soaked in dried blood. Blanket dark red. His worn 1911 on the ground like it fell from his hand. Eyes closed. Skin gray. Cold.
- He bled out in his sleep. Alone. Fighting to keep his eyes open until his body gave out.

**Jeremiah:** "...Why didn't you say something."

- Jeremiah sits on the floor next to the cot. Holds Charles's hand.
- Alma appears at the tent entrance. Doesn't scream. Doesn't cry. Grips the tent flap. Closes her eyes. Lips move but nothing comes out.

## Reactions

**Marcus:** Finds Jeremiah on the floor.
- Jeremiah: "He was shot in the ribs yesterday. He didn't tell anyone. He walked in, laid down, and bled to death. Alone."
- Marcus: "Why didn't he—"
- Jeremiah: "Because he's Charles. That's why."

**Joseph:** Brought to the tent by Luisa. Bad day. Fog clears briefly.
- "You were supposed to outlive all of us, you stubborn old bastard."
- Fog returns. By the time he reaches his tent, he's forgotten why he's crying.

## What They Find
- Blood-soaked rag Charles stuffed against the wound — tried to stop it himself
- Trail of blood drops from tent entrance to cot
- Under his pillow: a folded letter, old and worn

**"If you're reading this, I've stopped walking. Take care of each other. That's all I ever wanted. — Charles"**

He'd written it weeks or months ago. He knew the end was coming — COPD or otherwise.

## Charles's Legacy
- If he hadn't been shot, the COPD would have killed him in ~18 months
- He never told anyone about either — the bullet or the disease
- He carried everything alone until the very end
- 77 years old. The brain of the gang. The man who raised Jeremiah. Gone.
